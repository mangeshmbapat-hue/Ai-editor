## The colour rule (how colour is played)
1. **One grade family per act, and every act is warm.** The history act is sepia, bronze and black with lamp light; the paper beats are parchment and ink; the "today" footage is desaturated and slightly warm; the finale returns to bronze and gold. Nothing is cold-blue or neon.
2. **Red is the only loud colour,** and it means blood, anger or revolution: the giant red backdrop of the opening, the red letters "THE", "UNITE … THE MASSES" and "SILENT", the maroon quote card, crimson kinetic words, and the red circle that marks a detail. Red never appears as a decoration.
3. **Gold and cream are for meaning words** in the history act ("Revolution", "OVERTHROW", "UNJUST", "IT'S REAL MEANING"); **white** is for documents and quote cards.
4. **Real footage is graded to fit:** it gets a lower saturation, a warm cast, crushed blacks and a dark vignette, so modern phone video sits beside the sepia history scenes.

## Background
- **History act:** AI-made cinematic 1920s–30s scenes (a meeting by lantern light, a royal throne in a hall, a street crowd, a man writing at a wooden desk) in deep brown with **soft volumetric light, haze and dust**. The camera always drifts slowly (push-in or rise).
- **Paper beats:** old **parchment with a faint print texture**, used as a page the story is "written" on (a map, a news cutting, a pinned photo).
- **Type beats:** pure black with a soft crimson glow from below.
- **Today act:** real protest footage, often **inside a black letterbox** (a floating 16:9 window in the 9:16 frame) with a vignette, so it reads as "evidence".
- **Finale:** a painted poster: the hero, a sunset sky and a crowd of silhouettes before a parliament building.

## Text
- **Two type families, by meaning:**
  - **Condensed serif or display caps for history words**, in gold or cream with a soft glow ("Revolution", "OVERTHROW", "UNJUST SOCIAL ORDER", "IT'S REAL MEANING", "I AM PROUD OF YOU" in a condensed poster face).
  - **A clean sans for documents and today**: white or charcoal body text, crimson for emphasis.
- **Giant words behind people:** a huge word sits behind the hero and is partly hidden by him ("THE", "UNITE THE MASSES", "SILENT"). It is the main depth trick; the person cutout overlaps the word.
- **The headline says the VO's key word, in sync:** "Revolution" (2.1 s) → "is not the culture of bombs and pistols" (a small subline) → "IT'S REAL MEANING" (6.2 s) → "OVERTHROW" (7.7 s) → "UNJUST SOCIAL ORDER" (8.9–10 s) → "to make our country better" (14.5–15.4 s) → "be inspired by an ideology of human equality and socialism" (21.6–24.6 s) → "Today, protesting" (25.4 s) → "That's not dangerous" (29.4 s) → "what's / is accepting / everything / understand" (32.3–34.9 s).
- **Words build letter by letter or word by word, with a blur.** The serif headlines type in with a light flicker. The kinetic words on black slam in large and crimson while a **huge blurred "dangerous" sits behind them** as texture.
- **Document text is real-looking prose** with phrases **highlighted** (a red highlighter on the quote, a yellow highlighter on the news), so the eye goes to the exact words the VO says.
- **Small spaced caps captions** ("C R I T I C A L   S I L E N C E", "B H A G A T   S I N G H") act as section labels.

## How to merge cutouts and photos (the "merge" rules)
- **A cutout over a giant word:** the person always overlaps the big title text, which gives the picture depth.
- **A cutout breaking a frame:** inside a quote card, the hero's fist and shoulder go over the card's frame line. The frame becomes part of the composition, not a box around a photo.
- **Shared grade:** every cutout and photo is graded to the act's palette (sepia and brown for history, black-and-white for the quote card, warm and desaturated for today) before it is placed, with matching **grain** and **vignette**.
- **Soft light wrap:** the scene's light (lantern, haze) spills over the cutout's edges, so it sits in the scene and does not look pasted.
- **Prints and paper:** photos enter as **polaroids, pinned cards or peeling stickers** on parchment, so a modern photo becomes "evidence" on an old page.
- **Badges for ideas:** abstract ideas (bombs, pistols, reason, blind faith) are small **round cutout badges with a white ring**, sometimes struck through in red.
- **Floating windows for real footage:** modern video sits in a letterboxed window over black, with a soft edge and a vignette, never full-bleed next to the AI history scenes.

## Owner corrections (2026-11-14, Amit Shah explainer)
- **The giant word behind a person is the person's name** (NARENDRA MODI, AMIT SHAH), never a minor word.
- **Echo cutouts:** the same person repeated 4–5 times behind them, each one bigger, fainter and in black and white, fading back like shadows.
- **Readable type:**
  - headlines at least 7–9 % of the frame height
  - document lines at least 3.3 %
  - names of important people big and bold
- **People in one scene are treated as equals:** the same treatment and the same height.
- **Full-body cutouts are shown large,** at about 85–95 % of the frame height.

## The history effect (what makes it feel historical)
- A **sepia and bronze grade**, lantern and window light, haze, dust and film grain.
- **Old paper** textures, serif display type, pinned prints, the period costume (hats, waistcoats, suspenders) and period props (a lantern, a fountain pen, a pamphlet, a throne).
- A **slow, heavy camera**: push-ins and rises of 3–6 % per scene, never fast moves in the history act; speed comes only in the "today" act.
- A **deep, slow voice** with pauses, and music that swells under the history act and turns tense for today.
- **Then → now structure:** the history act sets an idea (the quote), and the today act shows it alive (real footage). The finale returns to the history look to close the loop.

## Pace
- History act: a new visual every **1.0–2.0 s**, always on the VO's key word.
- Kinetic type: one word per **0.4–0.6 s**.
- The today footage: clips of **0.8–2.5 s**, in a fast montage.

## 16:9 layout (the same style, landscape)
- **Giant word behind a person:** the word spans about 80 % of the frame's width; the person stands left- or right-of-centre over it instead of in the middle.
- **Quote card:** the card sits on one half with the cutout breaking its frame, and the quote text sits on the other half.
- **Paper beats:** the parchment fills the frame; the pinned photo sits on one third and the highlighted text on two thirds.
- **Real footage:** full-bleed with the warm grade and a vignette, or a large window (about 70 % of the width) over black.
- **Kinetic type:** one or two words per line at about 12 % of the frame height, with the blurred giant word behind at about 40 %.
- **Badges:** around the hero's head at about 9 % of the frame height.
