## The look: what makes it premium
1. **One strict palette per video, with three roles:**
   - P1: black, deep red and gold, with cream "paper" scenes for calm moments
   - P2: black, grey and white, with ONE hot accent per scene (red light, gold trophy, orange highlight)

   Nothing outside the palette ever appears, and the accent is used sparingly.
2. **Hero objects are sharp, HD and lit like product shots:**
   - a gold Spartan helmet, a gold brain, a gold bust in a baroque frame, a dollar bill, a traffic light, a glowing bulb, a gold trophy with a red cloth, a phone
   - Edges are crisp with no halo or fringe. Each object has real rim light, a soft contact shadow and depth.
   - The object is always large (40–60 % of the frame) and centred or placed by the composition. Never small and floating.
   - Many objects are 3D or AI renders matched to the palette, not random stock photos.
3. **Backgrounds belong to the subject's colour world:**
   - a black velvet void with soft red light (helmet)
   - a black studio (brain)
   - cream paper with gold flowers (bust)
   - a white wall with a diagonal light beam (bulb)
   - grey linen (trophy post)
   - a dark crowd render with one glowing figure (crowd)

   A soft vignette, film grain and slight depth-of-field blur sit on the background, never on the hero.
4. **Light is a design element:**
   - a soft red glow behind the helmet
   - a top light that blooms in
   - a **diagonal light sweep across a white wall** that passes over the bulb and the text
   - a bloom or whiteout used as a transition
5. **Editorial "poster" detailing:**
   - tiny micro-typography around the edges: issue numbers, dates, columns of small text, a barcode, crop marks, thin rules, a dotted progress row ("● ● ● ● ●")
   - a vertical giant word bleeding off the edge ("STAND")

   This makes every frame look like a magazine page. It is always small, low-contrast and never competes with the message.

## Text: what goes where
- **Fonts, mixed by role in the same line:**
  - a tall condensed bold sans in caps for punches: YOU DON'T NEED!, UNDERSTAND, ALL TRENDS, IDEAS
  - an elegant high-contrast serif for the voice line: stand out, Stop! copying, Start creating original, because, the crowd, Recognize
  - a light italic serif for asides: their, people instantly, "Viewers have no reason…"
  - a clean grotesk sans for statements: turn that attention., rewire, think
- **Text colours come only from the palette:**
  - P1: white and deep red (the key word in red: NEED!, UNDERSTAND, ACTION, right one)
  - P2: white, black and grey, plus the one accent
  - On cream, text is black with a red key word.
- **When text is transparent (no backing):** on clean, calm backgrounds (black voids, cream paper, a white wall), and for big statements.
- **When text gets a backing:**
  - a black highlight box swept in behind the key phrase ("doesn't reach everyone")
  - an orange marker bar behind "everyone else's"
  - a dark rounded pill for labels (ATTENTION, CURIOSITY, RETENTION around the brain)
  - a white card (profile card)

  Backings appear when the text sits over a busy image or when a phrase must be underlined in meaning.
- **Highlight with animation:**
  - The key word changes colour or gets a box AS it is spoken.
  - Words build letter by letter or word by word (typewriter in a grey "ghost" colour that turns solid).
  - A giant word sits behind as a texture ("st" in huge grey serif, "INTO ACTION" in red behind the white line).
- **Placement:**
  - top third for the headline
  - lower third for the statement under the hero
  - text often overlaps the hero slightly in front, or tucks behind it for depth ("or[bulb]ginal": the bulb sits in front of the word)

## Animation and transitions (every move eased, nothing shakes)
- **Text entrances:** a blur-to-sharp reveal, a slight scale settle, letters typing in grey and turning white, a sideways slide with motion blur.
- **Hero entrances:** a push-in from big to normal with focus pulling sharp (helmet). The object rises in with a shadow (trophy with red cloth). A 3D phone turns in from the edge.
- **Reveal by an object (the "paper door"):** two cream paper panels slide shut over a face, held by real hands. The headline is then written ON the closed door. Paper doors, cards and page flips work as wipes that carry the next text.
- **Camera:** slow push-ins on every scene, a parallax drift between foreground text and background, a rack focus from hero to text.
- **Transitions:**
  - a bloom or whiteout flash between colour worlds (black → cream)
  - a blur dissolve
  - a page or panel wipe
  - a match on shape (the money banner curls into the next scene)
  - a black frame (letterbox) closing in from the edges (bulb scene)
- **Ending:** a quote line in light italic on black, then a social end card (their own; we use the owner's CTA).

## How the Faceless Editor uses this (premium mode)
1. **Pick the palette first** from the topic: "power and attention" → black, red and gold; "creative and original" → monochrome with one accent. Hold it for the whole video.
2. **For every concrete noun in the VO, get a hero image that is sharp and HD** (at least 1080 px on its long side after the cutout), lit to match the palette.
   - Clean cutouts only: no holes, no halos, no stray background.
   - A gold or metal hero gets a rim light; a glowing object (bulb) gets a bloom.
   - If no sharp source exists, use a different concrete visual. Never a blurry or cheap one.
3. **Build each scene as a poster:**
   - a background from the subject's world (void, paper, wall, linen)
   - grain and a vignette
   - micro-typography details at the edges
   - the hero big
   - the line of text in the right font for its role, with the key word in the accent
4. **Choose the text treatment per line:**
   - open text on calm backgrounds
   - a highlight box or marker for the key phrase when meaning needs underlining
   - pills for labels around an object
   - a giant ghost word behind for the topic
5. **Motion:**
   - every scene pushes in slowly
   - the hero enters (rise, focus pull or 3D turn)
   - text blurs in or types in
   - the key word gets its colour or box on its spoken word
   - one light move per video at most (sweep or bloom)
   - transitions by bloom, blur or panel wipe
6. **Sound:** the music carries the swells; the samples have no SFX on text. Follow the owner rules for SFX and music.
